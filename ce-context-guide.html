Character Engine · reader's guide

What the model actually reads before it writes

Everything you type into Character Engine ends up somewhere in the text the model is handed. This page shows where. Open a row to see the real thing — the exact wording the engine produces, which screen you filled in to produce it, and how to write the field so it reads properly once it gets there. Many fields are dropped into the middle of a sentence the engine builds around them, so the form you write in matters. There is a summary of every one of those at the bottom, above the full example.

Standing instructions and world canon. These do not change from turn to turn.

2 · The reference shelf

Lorebook entries. Some are always on; the rest switch on when their subject is named or is in the room. NovelAI decides the order inside this block, with one exception — the engine forces the cast list to sit last, because it is the part that changes most often and the model weights recent text more heavily.

The Worldevery Location you have made Kesswater. A river town of eight hundred on both banks… Engine 
Places in this world.
Kesswater. A river town of eight hundred on both banks of the Ash, where
the sea road crosses.
The Salt Lantern. A two-storey inn at the bottom of Cooper's Hill, the only
building in town with glass in every window.
Common Room. Low-beamed, wide-hearthed, and never quite empty.

Where it comes from. Map The description field of every place you have made. A place with a description gets a sentence. A place without one gets its name in the closing list — so describing a place is what earns it room here.

What it does not say. Nothing about what is inside what. Where you put a place on the map is a layout choice, and the engine will not turn a layout choice into a claim about the world. If a place sits inside another one and that matters, write it in the description.

When it changes. Whenever you add, rename, describe or delete a place. Always on.

How to write the description Your text is placed after the name and a full stop, so it should read as the rest of a paragraph about that place — not as a heading or a label. A river town of eight hundred on both banks of the Ash. Town. Population 800. On the Ash.

Where it comes from. Assembled fresh every turn from seven sources, in the order below. Any one of them with nothing to say is left out entirely rather than printed empty.

It goes in the Author's Note, under a heading of its own. If you have written your own author's note, it stays above this and is never touched or overwritten.

The seven parts, in order
The objective, and where the encounter stands
Two facts in one paragraph. The Guild's factor is at the bar… is what you wrote when you started this encounter, and it never moves. The shape of a deal is on the table… is where the encounter's track currently sits, and it moves every time you roll. The last clause is how far away the thing you are aiming at is, and the engine writes that one. Threads · Actions · Settings › States
Both halves are their own sentence Write the objective as one plain sentence about what should happen next. Encounter band labels are also full sentences — present tense, stated as fact, because a fact is continued where an instruction is argued with. The shape of a deal is on the table and what is left is price Try not to close the deal yet
Today's date
Anything you put on the calendar for this exact day. Several notes on the same day are joined into one line. Settings › Time
A statement, not a reminder Nothing is added around it, so write what is true today rather than an instruction about it. The Guild's quarterly vote is today. Remember: vote day!
Time and place
The hour as a phrase rather than a clock reading, then where you are, then how the room is right now. That last sentence is the room's atmosphere for this hour — the same room reads differently at dawn. If you have not written any atmosphere, its plain description stands in. Turn the clock off and the time line disappears on its own. Map › Atmosphere · Settings › Time
Atmosphere follows a finished sentence It is appended after Wren Halloway is in Common Room. so it must stand on its own, with its own punctuation. Time bands are the opposite — they are dropped into It is … so they take a bare phrase. Rain on the shutters, the fire banked low.  — atmosphere late afternoon  — time band It is late afternoon.  — time band
Who is here
Anyone with something on their timetable gets a sentence saying what they are doing; everyone else is named in one list. Deliberately written as prose rather than a labelled roster — a heading in the context teaches the model to write headings back at you. Uses the same timetable text as the Character fold, so the -ing form serves both. Map · Profile › Schedule
What they are thinking
One line per person in the room: their current read on what is happening, and what they mean to do about it. You do not write these — the engine generates them from the profile and refreshes them on the interval you set. What shapes them is what you put in Core, Pursuit and the Resistance and Conduct slots. generated · Settings › Dynamics
What is between them
How two other people stand with each other, when both are present. Distinct from the Position line in a Character entry, which is one person's private view. This one is the shared history. Profile › Web
A phrase, after a dash It follows Ottis Vane & Sabra Quill — with nothing added, so write a phrase rather than a sentence about them. twenty years of trusting each other with letters and nothing else They have known each other twenty years.
What is pressing
A hook that has climbed high enough to demand attention, and how close it is to forcing itself. Low-level hooks stay quiet and colour the thinking lines instead. Placed last, nearest the writing. The urgency clause is added by the engine from the hook's level. Profile › Hooks
After the name and a colon Write the pressure itself, not who is under it — the name is already there. the sealed letter is addressed to the Guildmaster and she has not decided whether to deliver it Sabra has a letter she has not delivered.

Where it comes from. Actions Something crossed a line this turn. A roll pushed the encounter into a new band; a state bottomed out; you queued a note. When that happens, the engine has one sentence it needs the model to act on, and this is how it delivers it.

It arrives as if you had typed it. Not as a bracketed instruction — a bracket turns a storyteller into an assistant. Plain text, in your voice, as the last thing before the model begins.

Why the first line repeats the Scene State. On purpose. The Scene State sits thousands of words above this point; what actually paces a scene is the model reading the situation last.
